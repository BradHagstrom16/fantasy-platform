House Rules

Pick {{ scoring_slots }} sides a week against frozen lines, name one your Headliner for double, and drop your worst week before it counts.

The short version

Every week, you pick {{ scoring_slots }} sides: college or NFL, spreads or totals, whatever you like.

Then you name one of your {{ scoring_slots }} the Headliner. It is the pick you like most, and it scores double.

That is the whole game. Everything below just answers the question that comes next: "okay, but what happens if...?"

The week

How many picks
{{ scoring_slots }} sides a week, exactly. Any mix of college and NFL, any mix of spreads and totals.
One side per market
You can't take both sides of the same market. A spread pick and a total pick on the same game are two separate picks, not one.
The deadline
Saturday 11:00 AM CT. Your picks must land before 11:00:00; 10:59:59 is on time. Each game also locks at its own kickoff, so a Thursday game is committed Thursday while the rest of your sheet stays open.
Who sees what
Every sheet is sealed until each game kicks off. At kickoff, everyone's pick on that game shows on All Sheets; once the docket closes Saturday, every sheet is on the record, with the sides the clerk filed marked. The tiebreaker number shows when it locks.
If you file nothing
Miss the deadline with no sheet and the system fills one for you: favorites on the largest spreads and Overs on the highest totals, drawn only from games that kick off at or after the deadline. It never overwrites a pick you made, and it never files a reserve for you.

The Headliner

One of your {{ scoring_slots }} picks is your Headliner, and it scores double. You name it yourself, on any side you hold. You can't move it onto a game that has already kicked off, which closes the obvious exploit: naming Saturday's winner on Saturday morning.

Forget to name one? The system names it for you at the deadline: your largest favorite; if you backed no favorite, your highest-total Over; if you have neither, your earliest kickoff.

Held in reserve

Pick {{ backup_slot }} sits in reserve and normally scores nothing. If one of your first {{ scoring_slots }} picks is ruled No Contest, your reserve takes its place, filling the earliest-kickoff cancelled pick. If more than one is cancelled, only that first one is replaced; the rest are scored as pushes. If the cancelled pick was your Headliner, the reserve inherits the double.

No reserve, whether you never filed one or it was itself ruled No Contest? Then every cancelled pick that week is scored as a push.

The ledger

Your season standings, decided by three keys in order. Points decide it. Wins break a tie on points. The tiebreaker error breaks a tie on both, lowest first. Anyone level on all three shares a rank.

Key 1, points
The sum of your weekly points, minus your worst week. The drop starts once a second week is graded.
Key 2, wins
Your total winning picks. A Headliner win counts once here, not twice. Never dropped.
Key 3, the error account
Your tiebreaker error added up across the whole season, lowest first. Never dropped. The drop forgives points only, so a dropped week still counts toward your wins and your error.

The tiebreaker

One game a week is the designated tiebreaker, and everyone predicts its combined final score to the nearest tenth. Your error is the distance between your prediction and the real total: predict 47.3 on a game that finishes 44, and your error is 3.3. It adds up all season.

By rule the case is Monday Night Football, the last NFL game of the week. When two games play Monday the later kickoff holds it, and a week with no Monday game uses Sunday night. Week 1 has no NFL game, so its case is the last one on the college slate: SMU at Florida State on Labor Day. The Commissioner may move the case before the deadline; your number then resets to the new posted total, and you are told.

Skip it and you are charged the game's posted total as your prediction. That charge applies to everyone, every week, including weeks before you joined. Showing up late buys no advantage on this key.

The fine print

The rest of the rules exist because eventually someone will ask.

Overtime

Final score means final score. Everything grades on it, overtime included: spreads, totals, and the tiebreaker number.

An NFL tie

Graded normally against the number. A tie still beats a spread you were getting points on. On a pick'em, where the line is zero, a tie is a push.

A game thrown out

The Commissioner can rule a game No Contest, which is also what happens to a game postponed out of its week. Your reserve covers the first cancelled pick; any others are scored as pushes.

A bad line

A line imported wrong can be corrected, but only before the deadline and before that game kicks off, with a reason recorded. Everyone holding that market is notified so they can decide again. After the deadline it is not corrected: the game is ruled No Contest instead, and the reserve and push rules above take over.

The tiebreaker game is cancelled

If the designated tiebreaker game is cancelled before the deadline, a new one is designated and everyone's predictions reset to the new posted total. If it is cancelled after the deadline, no one is charged an error that week.

Where the lines come from

Lines are imported Tuesday morning and then frozen. The first fetch that finds a posted market locks it; later runs only fill markets that were still empty. A locked line never moves again, and your pick is graded against the number shown when you made it.

Each market takes the first book below that has posted it, on its own, so a game's spread and its total can come from different books. The book is recorded on the line and on your pick.
